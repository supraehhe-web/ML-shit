## 1. State of the project in one page

**Question.** Does diffusion-based hair removal (DM-AHR) change 7-class skin-lesion classification (ViT-B/16, HAM10000, 10,015 images) or explanation localization compared with original images and a classical DullRazor-style baseline?

**Arms.** A original; B DullRazor-style (morphology detector + Telea inpainting); A* original shrunk to 128 px and back (resolution control, applied to hair-present images only); C first diffusion run (defective trainer, kept as a recorded failure); Creleased the authors' released checkpoint (exploratory, added after the protocol was frozen); Cv2 rebuilt diffusion arm. Three seeds (42, 123, 2026), same recipe, same frozen lesion-grouped split.

**Headline (test macro-F1, full test n=1,502, mean ± SD over 3 seeds; see A1, A2):**

| Arm | Macro-F1 |
|---|---:|
| A original | 0.7500 ± 0.0049 |
| A* 128 px control | 0.7317 ± 0.0084 |
| Cv2 rebuilt diffusion | 0.7196 ± 0.0210 |
| Creleased (exploratory) | 0.7129 ± 0.0280 |
| B DullRazor | 0.6983 ± 0.0086 |
| C first diffusion run (defective) | 0.4261 ± 0.0130 |

**What I believe is supported by the evidence**
- The first diffusion run failed because my trainer gave the network the noise level in the wrong convention (`t/T`, which rises with t, where the sampler feeds `sqrt_alphas_cumprod_prev`, which falls). Measured epsilon errors, tests against upstream's loss, and the later recovery of usable output after the fix all point the same way (D25; `artifacts/logs/noise_convention_check.json`).
- After the fix, a sampler that starts from the noised hairy input at t0 of 600 or 1000 removes synthetic hair on validation pairs (about +9 dB over the hairy input, no run below it, A6).
- Original over DullRazor is a clear difference (0.052, 95% CI 0.019 to 0.087, Holm p 0.0036). Because DullRazor damages lesions on real hair (D31), this says little about hair removal in general.
- Cv2 cannot be told apart from A, A* or B in macro-F1 (A3c). Three seeds, seed SD 0.021.
- On the 186 synthetic test pairs, Cv2 (29.18 dB, SSIM 0.861) is clearly worse than DullRazor (35.73 dB, 0.918) and clearly better than the hairy input (22.13 dB, 0.520).

**What is not supported**
- That diffusion hair removal helps classification, localization or image fidelity on this cohort.
- That hair removal on real photographs is validated. The pre-registered Stage B gate failed (ratio 1.95 and 1.57 against 2.0; follow-up composite rule +0.14 and +0.15 against +0.20). Stage C was run anyway as a recorded deviation (D34). The only support for real-photo removal is my own visual review of 12 dense-hair validation photos and a ground-truth test on pasted real strands.

**Where it stands now.** A full-resolution paste-back variant (Stage D) was meant to fix the resolution cap. On validation synthetic pairs it reached 33.33 dB but SSIM 0.846 against a 0.90 bar, so it failed its pre-registered gate. I stopped. No Cv2p image was built for real or test data. The owner is deciding between the options in section 11.

**Project gates (as of commit f2018d8):** acceptance 14/14, deliverable 8/8, CPU unit tests 61 passed and 3 skipped (latest run). The Word/PDF reports, README, code guide and final report were updated for Stage C. They do not mention Stage D yet.

## 4. The pipeline and the code

**Models**
- ViT: torchvision `vit_b_16` ImageNet-1k V1, 224 px input, 7 logits. AdamW lr 3e-5, weight decay 0.05, 5 warm-up epochs then cosine, batch 32, up to 100 epochs, early stopping on validation macro-F1 with patience 10, inverse-frequency class weights, augmentation: RandomResizedCrop(scale 0.75 to 1.0), flips, 15 degree rotation, mild colour jitter. Evaluation transform: resize 255 then centre crop 224 (D23). The recipe is identical across arms.
- Diffusion: DM-AHR is an SR3-style conditional DDPM (upstream pinned at commit `0aee1bb`, vendored in `third_party/dmahr`): U-Net in 6 / out 3 channels, 64 inner channels, multipliers 1/2/4/8/8; 2,000-step linear schedule 1e-6 to 1e-2; 128 px native; L1 loss on predicted epsilon; Adam 1e-4, batch 4, gradient clip 1.0, **no EMA** in my trainer. Input is the hairy image as condition, target the clean image.
- Released checkpoint sha256 `c99e406050d3aaf36473f76477bcfb8ba05d80a420d50d57533eac75b8c8d2fe`. The authors' 25-step sampling routine that produced their images is not in the committed code (D26); **NOT VERIFIED** how it was run.

**Code layout.** Library code in `src/dm_ahr_vit/` runs on CPU without Modal (`config`, `paths`, `data/{dataset,hair_composite,synthetic_hair}`, `hair/{screening,dullrazor,harvest,pasteback}`, `diffusion/{dmahr_adapter,train}`, `vit/train`, `stats/bootstrap`, explainability). `modal_app.py` is a thin wrapper that mounts the repo into an image and a volume. ## 6. Findings by stage (tables in the appendix)

### 6.1 Why the first diffusion run failed, and what was ruled out
- Inverted noise-level encoding (section 5.3). Measured: under the sampler's convention the released checkpoint's epsilon error is 2.9x to 20.5x better at every timestep, whereas my old model was about 10x better under `t/T` (`noise_convention_check.json`). Zeroing the condition image changed the released model's error by 3.3x to 4.8x but my old model's by 0.97x to 1.8x, so the old model barely used the hair condition.
- Not explained by sampler precision (TF32 confound identified and withdrawn). A single validation pair sampled with 16 noise draws on the repaired 20k model gave 6.5 to 20.0 dB in true fp32 against 25.2 to 27.4 dB for the released checkpoint, which showed the pure-noise sampling chain was unstable for the repaired model.
- After the fix and 50,000 iterations, high-noise behaviour was still weaker than the released model's (A12: epsilon error at t=1500 and t=1900 of 0.0086 and 0.0101 at 50k against 0.0048 and 0.0038). **The 12-pair, one-draw profile is too small to call that flat or improving**: at t=1500 the error fell from 0.0098 (20k) to 0.0086 (50k), at t=1900 it did not. Sampling quality was never measured after 20,000 iterations on the pure-noise chain. The gradient-norm check shows the repaired 20k model's gradient norms of 0.7 to 1.9 against 0.18 to 0.39 for the released checkpoint, with 62.5% of draws above the clip of 1.0 (A15, two draws per level).

### 6.2 Stage A: partial-noise sampler (A6)
Start the strided DDIM chain (50 steps over the 2,000-step schedule) from the *noised hairy input* at schedule index t0 instead of pure noise. For the 50k model on validation pairs: t0=600 and 1000 give about +9 dB over the hairy-input floor with no run below it; t0=1500 has 13 to 14% of runs below the floor; a pure-noise start gives about -5 dB with over 90% of runs below. The released checkpoint as positive control at a pure-noise start scored +1.96 dB then +1.78 dB against a pre-registered bar of +2.0, so **Stage A is recorded as not validated under the strict rule**. I argued the bar was probably too tight for that pair set but did not change it.

### 6.3 Stage B0 and B: real hair (A7, A8)
- B0 (train photos, n=40): ratio DullRazor 21.73, released 1.13, 50k model 1.71 at t0=600, 1.45 at t0=1000, 1.09 at t0=1500. Lesions were intact in the model outputs but thick real strands remained.
- Harvest: dark thin long strands cut from real hair-present train and validation photos with a rule set I tightened after seeing lesion texture, pen marks and frame edges harvested in 5 of 8 reviewed images. Full run: 5,996 candidate photos, 1,893 yielded strands (train 1,521, validation 372). Composites later restrict sources to layers covering at least 0.4% of the image after a review of 24 random and 12 densest layers found contamination in the small ones (3 of 24 random layers contaminated). Both rules were fitted by eye (W9).
- Fine-tune: 20,000 iterations from the 50k snapshot on composites (1,152 train targets, 722 usable train sources; 30% of samples also get the old simple synthetic hair). One preemption resumed from 9,000; one loss spike of 0.51 at 14,100 recovered. Validation composite loss 0.0150 at 300 iterations, 0.0135 at 20,000.
- **Gate (validation, 40 real photos, frozen detector at 128 px)**: ratio must reach 2.0 at t0 of 600 or 1000. Result 1.95 and 1.57, up from 1.69 and 1.46 for the 50k model on the same photos. **Failed.**
- Ground-truth composite measure (pre-registered after the failure): hair removal 0.77 and 0.81 for v2 against 0.64 and 0.66 for the 50k model. The rule needed +0.20; the gain was +0.14 and +0.15. **Not met.**
- Dense-hair look (12 validation photos with the most harvested hair; agent-reviewed, not expert-reviewed): v2 removes nearly all visible hair with lesion outline and colour kept; leaves some smudges where hair is long, parallel and dense; lesions lose fine texture because the model works at 128 px. The detector shows all 12 photos under half their original coverage.
- I argue the ratio instrument cannot grade a diffusion model because the model changes pixels everywhere, which is why I went ahead. A reviewer may reasonably disagree (W1).

### 6.4 Stage C: the rebuilt arm Cv2, one test touch (A1 to A5)
Frozen before test use (`reports/protocol_addendum_stageC.md`): checkpoint `dmahr_v2/final_gen.pth`, 50 strided DDIM steps from the noised input at index 600, fp32, 128 px. t0=600 was chosen because it scored better than 1000 on three of four validation measures. All 8,993 hair-present images were replaced by the 128 px output, the 1,022 hair-absent images kept as the original. A validation check came first: 29.02 dB against 22.08 dB (+6.94, bar +5).
- Classification: Cv2 0.7196 ± 0.0210. A minus Cv2 +0.031 (95% CI -0.006 to +0.066, Holm p 0.198); B minus Cv2 -0.022 (CI -0.052 to +0.009, Holm p 0.198); Cv2 minus A* -0.012 (Holm p 0.386). Hair-present subset similar. The secondary Wilcoxon test on per-lesion correctness gives Holm p 0.027 for B against Cv2 and 0.034 for Cv2 against A*, with effect sizes near 0.05; I did not treat them as findings.
- Image quality: Cv2 29.18 dB / 0.861 against DullRazor 35.73 / 0.918 (difference -6.55 dB, CI -6.91 to -6.19).
- Explanation overlap against lesion masks: Grad-CAM Cv2 0.410 against A* 0.406 (difference +0.004, CI -0.006 to +0.014, p 0.43). Rollout Cv2 0.604 against A* 0.580 (+0.024, Holm p below 0.0001), but that interval resamples lesions after averaging seeds, so it leaves seed variance out, and Cv2 beats A* in two of three seeds (A5).
- Reading: the rebuilt arm sits where its 128 px resolution puts it. It is not better than A*, so nothing here shows an effect of hair removal.

### 6.5 Stage D: paste-back (A9)
Keep original full-resolution pixels everywhere and replace only pixels where the diffusion fill is brighter than the input (dark hair removed), optionally limited to a thin full-resolution dark-hair mask. Gates D1 to D5 were pre-registered before the first run (D6 was added with the second variant); variant `coarse` best 31.76 dB / SSIM 0.831 (fails D1 at 32.02 and D2 at 0.90); variant `thin` (registered after the first failure) best 33.33 dB / SSIM 0.846 (passes D1, fails D2). Best SSIM anywhere in the thin grid was 0.847, below Cv2 alone (0.858) and DullRazor (0.915). All best settings sat at the smallest grid value. My reading: the upsampled 128 px fill does not match local texture and tone at the seam; a wide band blurs, a narrow one leaves hair edges. Two tuning rounds on the same 182 validation pairs add some risk of fitting to it.

## 10. Specific questions for you

1. **Gate discipline.** Was proceeding to Stage C after the Stage B gate failed defensible, given the written reasons in D34? Would you down-weight every Cv2 result?
2. **Sampler mismatch (W11).** Is starting from a noised hairy image at t0=600 principled enough? Would starting from a noised first-pass estimate, or training with noised-hairy inputs, be cleaner?
3. **Conditioning.** I measured how much the old model used the condition image. I did **not** re-measure it for the repaired or fine-tuned model. Should that be done before any claim about "mask-free" behaviour?
4. **High-noise capacity.** What explains the gap at t of 1500 to 1900 against the released checkpoint (0.0086 and 0.0101 against 0.0048 and 0.0038)? Candidates: 725,000 released iterations against 50,000 plus 20,000; about 9,300 released training images against 654 pairs; no EMA in my trainer; gradient clipping engaged on most draws; batch 4. I tried snapshot averaging as an EMA proxy (30k to 50k) with a minor effect. I did not verify whether upstream trains with an EMA copy and whether the released weights are the EMA weights. **NOT VERIFIED.**
5. **Optimizer settings.** Would a longer run with EMA, or a lower learning rate, or no clipping, plausibly fix high-noise behaviour, so that pure-noise sampling (the paper's method) becomes usable?
6. **Measurement.** For real hair without ground truth, what instrument would you trust? Candidates: an independent hair segmentation (the ChimeraNet masks named in the proposal were not obtained), blinded human ratings, a classifier-free proxy. The inside/outside ratio and the detector coverage are mine and have known flaws.
7. **Statistics.** Are the lesion-group bootstrap with seed-averaging, the three Holm families (planned, secondary control, exploratory) and the Wilcoxon on per-group correctness the right design? What would you report instead of `dz` (W7)? How many seeds would make a 0.02 macro-F1 difference resolvable?
8. **Train/test shift in the diffusion arms.** Hair-present ViT training images go through a diffusion model that was never trained on them as inputs; its clean targets were hair-absent images, which are passed through unchanged. I think the shift is mild, but please check my reasoning.
9. **Hair-present definition (W6).** Should the study stratify or restrict to images with real hair burden (for example detector coverage above a higher cut) before judging hair removal?
10. **A* adequacy.** A* shrinks only hair-present images to 128 px, matching Cv2's resolution profile. Is that the right control, or should hair-absent images be shrunk too?
11. **Stage D.** Is my reading (the 128 px fill is the limit, not the mask) right? Would you accept a hybrid that uses diffusion as the hair/lesion discriminator and classical full-resolution inpainting as the fill, under the label "hybrid"?
12. **Overall.** If you were the client, would you accept "no measurable effect of hair removal on classification; diffusion not better on image fidelity; real-photo removal unvalidated" as the deliverable, or is there an experiment that should be run first?
